Description

The PastScape record describes Tewkesbury Abbey as a Benedictine abbey and the Church of St Mary's Abbey. The abbey was founded in 1102 and the church consecrated in 1121. The central tower was added during the second quarter of the twelfth century, and the church suffered serious fire damage in 1178. The north transept was altered in the early thirteenth century and modified again later in that century. The east arm was extensively rebuilt in the early fourteenth century, with the stone vaulting completed by the middle of the century.

After the Dissolution the eastern Lady Chapel and monastic buildings were removed, while the surviving abbey church continued as the parish church. Later alterations included rebuilding of the west window in 1686, demolition of the detached northern belfry in 1817, and major restoration by Sir Gilbert Scott and Sons between 1875 and 1879. Galleries at the crossing were removed in 1909.

The record also notes the traditional possibility of an earlier monastery at Tewkesbury, perhaps founded about 715, destroyed in the ninth century and later re-established as a cell of Cranborne Abbey around 980. In 1102 Tewkesbury became independent and Cranborne became its dependency. Recorded dependencies included Bristol, Cardiff, Cranborne, Deerhurst, Goldcliff, Llantwit Major and an almshouse at Tewkesbury.
